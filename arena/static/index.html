03 / LEARN THROUGH PLAY

PPO training

FROM A BC CHECKPOINT

Start from a completed Behaviour Cloning student, then improve its policy through poker rewards. Train against registered bots, copies of the learner, or a mixture. One episode is one table hand with one learner. Evaluation hands are separate from the training episode target.

View saved PPO / self-play reward graphs →

Choose a completed BC checkpoint.
Who should the agent train against?

Train against the registered bots selected below. The same fixed pool supplies validation opponents.

4 players = learner + 3 copies; 5 players = learner + 4; 6 players = learner + 5. Copies use identical weights with independent cards, histories and random action choices. They refresh after each PPO iteration and remain fixed during a rollout. Only the learner supplies training decisions; its seat rotates.

Self-play chip profit can stay near zero while both sides improve. Fixed external opponents measure playing strength and select the best saved policy in every mode.

What should the agent learn to maximize?

Train on placement after each full 100-hand game. Validation uses the competition's duplicate-round placement, with the same small chip-profit component.

Competition standings use round-placement points across four rounds. This training objective targets game placement; validation measures the duplicate-round step. Official scoring rules

ALTERNATIVE / LEARN FROM MATCHED ATTEMPTS

GRPO training

FROM A BC CHECKPOINT

Start from a completed Behaviour Cloning checkpoint and compare several attempts at the same game. GRPO learns from relative rewards within each group without a value critic. One episode is one table hand. This is a separate experiment; use held-out poker results to compare it with PPO.

CPU preview workspace. GRPO experiments and saved snapshots stay in this preview.
Choose a completed BC checkpoint.
What should the agent learn to maximize?

Train on placement after each full 100-hand game. Validation uses the competition's duplicate-round placement, with the same small chip-profit component.

Competition standings use round-placement points across four rounds. This training objective targets game placement; validation measures the duplicate-round step. Official scoring rules

Advanced training and evaluation settings
Milestone evaluations

Policies are saved at exact milestone episode counts, after complete groups. Episode targets, rollouts and milestone intervals must be multiples of the group size × 100 hands. Every evaluation game has 100 hands; milestone results and training validation are separate comparisons.

Choose a completed checkpoint and at least five opponents for all table sizes.

Opponent pool

Milestone evaluations require at least five distinct opponents because they always include 4-, 5- and 6-player tables. When milestones are off, choose enough distinct opponents to fill your training tables. Each hand has one learner.

What the group teaches

With four attempts, one group uses 4 × 100 = 400 training episodes. Decks, seats and opponent setup match within that group; only the learner action-sampling seed changes. Opponent responses can still differ as play changes.

A group with identical rewards gives no relative reward learning signal. The plots show group reward spread and the share of groups with no spread.

Training best is selected by weighted duplicate-round placement and chip score on fixed validation games. The initial BC policy can remain best.

Milestone best compares the separate milestone evaluations. Game placement and duplicate-round placement are shown separately. Download a saved policy while a local job is running, paused or completed.

A separate held-out test evaluates the selected policy after training. Compare placement, chips and failures on fresh games. GRPO is not guaranteed to outperform PPO; neither training loss nor reward spread establishes playing strength.

Pause preserves completed iterations. An unfinished iteration is replayed when you resume.
